Planning evidence only; no criterion or launch approval. [#172's final boundary](https://github.com/kristianelmer/Talli/issues/172#issuecomment-5423704900), freshly read, accepts ordinary cash capital increases and clear registered loss-covering reductions. This refines [the September 23 gap record](capital-source-remaining-scope-20260923.md).

Primary-source findings checked October 9:

- Brønnøysundregistrene's current [AS reduction guidance](https://www.brreg.no/aksjeselskap/aksjekapital/nedsettelse-av-aksjekapital/) states a three-year dividend restriction following loss coverage without creditor notice. Its [increase guidance](https://www.brreg.no/aksjeselskap/aksjekapital/forhoyelse-av-aksjekapital/) describes new shares or increased existing nominal value, cash contributions, decisions, amended articles, contribution confirmation and register updates. This supports a separate cash-nominal subtype; the exact decision and consent requirements still need an explicit current rule mapping.
- Current [Skatte-ABC A-6-7.6.2 and A-6-7.7](https://oppslag.rettskilder.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder2/type/handboker/skatte-abc/gjeldende/skatteabc-A-6), retrieved through the official indexed text after direct-fetch timeout, describe fund-issued capital being reduced first and preservation/recovery of the tax paid-in position after loss write-down without repayment. Fund-origin amounts can themselves derive from paid-in or earned equity; source history matters. The pinned [RF main XSD, post 16](https://github.com/kristianelmer/Talli/blob/5595cc03e6439bc223134d5ffe03225d9f474898/docs/filing/aksjonaerregisteroppgaveHovedskjema.xsd#L4773) separately reports paid-in reduction 17717 and fund-issued reduction 17721. A no-payout registered reduction therefore must not mechanically subtract from tax paid-in balances.
- **Current-law access resolved:** the [October 9 Lovdata capture](capital-law-source-20261009.json) contains current §§12-5, 12-6 and 18-4 from the ordinary public browser, with verified normalized-text hashes and full-document header metadata. Direct fetch still returned405; this no longer blocks source review. Section12-5(1) restricts the **dividend decision**, starting from registration, with capital-restoration and creditor-procedure exceptions. Section18-4(2) gives calendar-year/date calculation. The [bounded rule mapping](capital-restriction-rule-mapping-20261009.json) distinguishes those statutory facts from admission choices and unresolved boundaries. The original government proposal remains historical corroboration, not a substitute for current law.

For the prior-year contract, compute each qualifying reduction's calendar end date as its registration date plus three years, clamping 29 February to 28 February where necessary; never use 1095 days, year-end or browser/UTC conversion. Assess the actual dividend decision date, not payment, filing, approval-read or today's date. Carry each reduction independently across income years; a later reduction cannot erase or restart another record. A restriction result clears only when every applicable reduction is resolved and the relevant history is complete.

**Bounded admission mapping, not a new statutory restriction:** dates before that end date remain restricted unless a verified exception already applies. Dates safely after the end date can use elapsed time. The exact end-day comparison remains unresolved by the inspected primary materials; if it falls on a non-working day, §18-4(3)'s applicability to this waiting restriction also needs resolution. Until clarified, classify only that boundary interval (end date through any potentially applicable next working day) as `unknown`, rather than silently choose `>=` or assert an extra legal waiting day. Ordinary dates outside that interval, leap-day calculation, history persistence and rejection paths can proceed. A reduction/clearance and dividend recorded on the same date also require evidence of their order; no invented timestamps.

Early clearance must bind completed evidence to the specific restriction: an effective registered capital increase of at least the reduction (cash/premium alone is insufficient), or verified completion of the applicable §12-6 procedure. Six weeks alone, a filed notice, board intent or a customer `cleared` flag is insufficient. A single ordinary registered cash increase with an unambiguous reduction/decision chronology is the initial restoration case; shared increases across multiple reductions, interleaved reductions, fund/noncash conversions and §12-5(2)'s simultaneous route need separate mapping. Those ambiguities block only affected exception claims; multiple reductions can still clear independently by elapsed time. Missing or contradictory history cannot become `clear`, and evidence changes must invalidate dependent approval without erasing historical facts.
