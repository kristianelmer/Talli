# Lawful agent-led recruitment for Talli's validation group

Status: official-source research complete

Research date: 2026-08-26

Applies to: recruiting Norwegian holding AS companies into Talli's free
pre-launch validation group through asynchronous channels

## Decision

Talli can recruit the validation group with an operational agent, but the agent
must use an **opt-in-first public funnel**:

1. Publish a Talli application page with clear participation terms and privacy
   information.
2. Publish clearly identified recruitment posts on Talli's own public channels
   and in communities that allow commercial posts.
3. Ask relevant partners to publish the same public application link to their
   audiences without giving Talli anyone's contact details.
4. Reply to people who apply or explicitly ask to hear from Talli.
5. Only if that funnel is insufficient, use a separately approved, tightly
   limited campaign to genuine generic company inboxes such as `post@company.no`.

The agent must not cold-email, text, or direct-message a named person. This is
true even when the person is contacted in a business role or their details are
published in Brønnøysundregistrene or another public directory. Public details
are not consent.

The invitation should be treated as marketing. Calling it research, a survey,
or a free offer does not safely remove it from the marketing rules: the legal
threshold for marketing is low, and an electronic survey that also promotes
the sender is covered. A request sent to obtain marketing consent is itself a
marketing message. Sources: [Marketing Control Act section
15](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2009-01-09-2/%C2%A715),
[Datatilsynet's newsletter, email-list and SMS guidance, last changed 6 May
2026](https://www.datatilsynet.no/personvern-pa-ulike-omrader/kundehandtering-handel-og-medlemskap/nyhetsbrev-epostlister-og-sms/),
and [Forbrukertilsynet's electronic-marketing
guide](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Veiledning-om-markedsf%C3%B8ring-via-epost-sms-ol.pdf),
sections 2.4-2.7.

## Practical channel matrix

`Allowed` still means that the baseline controls below must be met. `Conditional`
means the agent needs proof of the stated condition before acting. `Prohibited`
means the channel is unavailable for this recruitment effort; a new legal
decision would be required to change it.

| Channel or action | Status | Conditions and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talli-owned public application page | **Allowed** | Let the person choose to apply. Show who Talli is, what the validation group involves, the free-of-charge period, any required feedback, selection criteria, data use, retention, and withdrawal route before submission. Collect only what selection and onboarding need. |
| Public posts on Talli's own social profiles | **Allowed** | Make the commercial purpose and Talli identity obvious. Link to the application page; do not invite people to publish personal or company-confidential details in comments. |
| Posts in relevant public or private communities | **Allowed with community permission** | The group must allow commercial/recruitment posts or an administrator must approve the post. Identify it as Talli recruitment/advertising. The agent may answer public factual questions, but must move applications to the private Talli form. |
| Partner or association publishes Talli's link in its public channel | **Allowed** | The partner publishes a clearly identified post to a general audience. No lead list or named contact details are transferred to Talli. Any benefit paid to the partner or referrer must be disclosed where required. |
| Inbound application or explicit request for contact | **Allowed** | Reply only about the requested validation-group process. Record the request, channel, date, and scope. Operational messages needed to evaluate or run the application are not a licence for unrelated marketing. Use a separate optional consent for later launch/news marketing. |
| Genuine generic AS inbox, for example `post@`, `firmapost@`, or `regnskap@` | **Conditional** | Section 15 protects physical persons; Datatilsynet's current guidance says the consent rule applies when marketing is sent to a specific person, including an employee. A generic company address falls outside that personal-recipient condition. The agent must verify that the address is genuinely role-based, target the AS rather than an owner, use no named-person enrichment, clearly identify the message as marketing from Talli, offer a simple opt-out, and follow the Talli limit of one reminder. If ownership of the address is unclear, do not send. |
| Named work email, such as `firstname@company.no` | **Conditional on prior Talli-specific consent or an explicit request** | A worker is a physical person even when contacted about work. A public listing or relevant job title is not consent. The existing-customer exception should not be used for this pre-launch group unless a later review proves every statutory condition. |
| Personal email or SMS | **Conditional on prior Talli-specific consent or an explicit request** | The same prior-consent rule applies. A cold message asking the person to consent is not a workaround. |
| LinkedIn, Facebook, Slack, Discord, forum, or other private/direct message to a person | **Conditional on prior Talli-specific consent or an explicit request** | Forbrukertilsynet treats social-media direct messages as individual electronic marketing covered by the spam rule. A public profile or group membership is not consent. |
| Partner emails or messages its own list about Talli | **Conditional** | The partner must be able to prove that its recipients' consent or another applicable exception covers this message and channel. Talli must not assume that consent to the partner covers third-party marketing. The preferred route is a non-personal public post linking to Talli's form. |
| Warm referral or introduction | **Conditional** | The referrer may give the person Talli's public link. Talli may contact the person only after the person asks for the introduction or gives documented consent to Talli contact. Do not accept uploaded or forwarded lists of friends, owners, or clients. |
| Fixed incentive for a participant or referrer | **Conditional** | Prefer a fixed, clearly described benefit over a prize draw. State all conditions, including feedback duties and the exact free-of-charge period. Do not call participation simply "free" if the person must also accept unrelated marketing or undisclosed obligations. Do not reward disclosure of another person's contact details. |
| Paid public ads | **Conditional** | Legally possible as public advertising if Talli and the commercial purpose are clear. Tracking, audience targeting, and cookies need their own privacy basis. Any ad spend needs Kristian's separate cost approval before use. |
| Scraped or bought named lead list | **Prohibited** | Public or purchased data does not prove section 15 consent, and it creates GDPR source, legal-basis, expectation, transparency, accuracy, and objection problems. Do not upload such a list to an agent or outreach tool. |
| Cold named-person email, SMS, or social DM asking for consent | **Prohibited** | Forbrukertilsynet treats the consent request itself as marketing. No message may be sent in order to manufacture the prior consent the first message already required. |
| Agent posing as a customer, community member, or Kristian | **Prohibited** | Marketing and its sender must be identifiable. The agent acts for Talli and must never hide that fact or create fake grassroots interest. |
| Contact after opt-out, objection, or withdrawal | **Prohibited** | GDPR gives an unconditional right to object to direct marketing. Marketing processing must then stop. Keep only the minimum suppression evidence needed to prevent another contact. |

The business-versus-person distinction above comes from the wording of section
15 and Datatilsynet's current example: a named worker address is a specific
physical person. Forbrukertilsynet's guide expressly distinguishes such an
address from a non-personal address such as `post@company.no`. The sender bears
the risk when an address's ownership is unclear. Sources: [Marketing Control
Act section 15](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2009-01-09-2/%C2%A715),
[Datatilsynet guidance](https://www.datatilsynet.no/personvern-pa-ulike-omrader/kundehandtering-handel-og-medlemskap/nyhetsbrev-epostlister-og-sms/),
and [Forbrukertilsynet's electronic-marketing
guide](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Veiledning-om-markedsf%C3%B8ring-via-epost-sms-ol.pdf),
sections 2.4-2.5. Forbrukertilsynet also identifies email, SMS, and social-media
direct messages as channels normally requiring consent in its [competition
guidance](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Veileder-for-bruk-av-konkurranser.pdf).

## Baseline controls for every channel

### Message and sender

Every public post or direct message must:

- say that it is recruitment/marketing for Talli where the context does not
  already make that unmistakable;
- identify Talli and the legal sender, and give a working contact route;
- describe the free validation offer and its conditions accurately;
- avoid promises about launch price, filing coverage, results, or future access
  that Talli cannot yet prove;
- link to the privacy information; and
- provide a simple, free way to say "stop" when the message is direct.

Electronic marketing must make clear whose behalf it is sent on, and an
unsolicited email must be recognizable as marketing when received. Discounts,
gifts, and similar offers and their conditions must be clear. Source:
[Electronic Commerce Act section
9](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2003-05-23-35/%C2%A79). Marketing must
also be presented as marketing, and its commercial purpose must not be hidden.
Sources: [Marketing Control Act section
3](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2009-01-09-2/%C2%A73) and [section
8](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2009-01-09-2/%C2%A78).

### Consent

Where consent is required, it must be freely given, specific, informed, and
unambiguous, recorded through an affirmative action, and as easy to withdraw as
to give. It must say that **Talli** may contact the person, name the channels and
purpose, and not hide the choice inside terms or use a pre-ticked box. Talli
must be able to prove the exact consent later. Source: [GDPR article
7](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-38/gdpr/ARTIKKEL_7) and
[Datatilsynet's guidance](https://www.datatilsynet.no/personvern-pa-ulike-omrader/kundehandtering-handel-og-medlemskap/nyhetsbrev-epostlister-og-sms/).

Application contact and future marketing are separate purposes. A person may
ask Talli to contact them about the validation group without consenting to
launch announcements or later sales messages. Participation in the free group
must not be conditional on unrelated marketing consent.

### GDPR legal basis, public sources, and transparency

Each processing purpose needs a recorded legal basis:

- consent for personal electronic marketing where section 15 requires it;
- documented legitimate interest for the minimum named business-contact data
  needed to assess and administer an application submitted for a company (use
  contract only where the individual is actually party to that contract); or
- a documented legitimate-interest assessment where that basis is genuinely
  necessary and no stricter marketing rule blocks the contact.

Legitimate interest is not permission to cold-message a person in a channel
that section 15 prohibits. It requires a lawful, real, defined purpose;
necessity; and a balance that favours Talli after considering the person's
reasonable expectations and a less intrusive route. The assessment should be
written before processing starts. Sources: [GDPR article
6](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-38/gdpr/ARTIKKEL_6) and
[Datatilsynet's legitimate-interest guidance, last changed 17 March
2026](https://www.datatilsynet.no/rettigheter-og-plikter/virksomhetenes-plikter/om-behandlingsgrunnlag/nodvendig-for-a-ivareta-legitime-interesser---interesseavveiing/).

Public availability does not switch GDPR off. If Talli obtains personal data
from a public register, website, partner, or referral, it must tell the person
the categories and source—including that it was public—plus the purpose, legal
basis, retention, recipients, and rights. If the data will be used to contact
the person, that information is due no later than the first communication.
Source: [GDPR article
14](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-38/gdpr/ARTIKKEL_14) and
[Datatilsynet's right-to-information guidance](https://www.datatilsynet.no/rettigheter-og-plikter/den-registrertes-rettigheter/rett-til-informasjon/).

Only collect data needed for selection and participation, keep it only as long
as needed, and do not add email-open, click, or cross-site tracking by default.
Datatilsynet treats such tracking as separate processing needing its own legal
basis. Sources: [GDPR article
5](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-38/gdpr/ARTIKKEL_5) and
[Datatilsynet's email-list guidance](https://www.datatilsynet.no/personvern-pa-ulike-omrader/kundehandtering-handel-og-medlemskap/nyhetsbrev-epostlister-og-sms/).

### Objections, opt-outs, and follow-up limits

The right to object to direct marketing must be highlighted clearly and
separately at the first communication. Once a person objects, Talli must stop
using their data for direct marketing. Source: [GDPR article
21](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-38/gdpr/ARTIKKEL_21).

Norwegian law does not set a useful numeric follow-up allowance. Talli should
therefore adopt these conservative operating limits:

- genuine generic company inbox: one initial message and at most one reminder
  7-14 days later;
- consented personal recipient: stay inside the stated purpose, channel, and
  frequency, with at most one unanswered recruitment reminder;
- inbound applicant: only messages reasonably needed to process the
  application and run the agreed validation work; and
- any refusal, opt-out, objection, withdrawal, bounce showing a personal
  address, or uncertainty about consent/address type: stop immediately.

Silence, a message open, a link click, public profile activity, or membership
in a relevant group is not consent.

### Required records

Before the agent sends or publishes anything, Talli needs a small compliance
record that can prove:

- the approved audience rule, channel, message template, sender identity, and
  send/follow-up limit;
- for direct email, the source and classification of each address as generic
  company inbox, consented personal address, or explicit inbound request;
- for consent, who consented, when, how, to what exact Talli purpose and
  channels, the text/version shown, and any withdrawal;
- for legitimate interest, the purpose, necessity, less intrusive alternatives,
  reasonable expectations, balance, safeguards, and approval date;
- the privacy notice version and source information supplied;
- sends, replies, reminders, objections, withdrawals, and suppression status;
  and
- partner approval and the partner's proof of authority for any direct message
  it sends on Talli's behalf.

Do not put lead data, application data, or message content containing personal
data in Git, GitHub issues, general agent transcripts, or analytics tools.
Before an external agent or outreach provider processes personal data for
Talli, verify its role, retention, training use, security, processing location,
subprocessors, and transfer basis, and sign a data-processing agreement where
it acts as Talli's processor. Datatilsynet states that a business using a
marketing provider to send on its behalf needs such an agreement. Source:
[Datatilsynet's data-processing agreement
guidance](https://www.datatilsynet.no/rettigheter-og-plikter/virksomhetenes-plikter/hvordan-lage-en-databehandleravtale/).

## Automation disclosure

As of 26 August 2026, the EU AI Act has not been implemented in Norwegian law.
The Norwegian government said on 4 August 2026 that a revised consultation was
planned and that it intended to present the Norwegian AI law to Parliament in
spring 2027. Current Norwegian marketing and privacy rules still apply to AI
use. Source: [Norwegian Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance, 4
August
2026](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/tung-vil-sende-ki-loven-med-endringer-pa-horing/id3169693/).

There is therefore no identified current Norwegian rule requiring every
human-reviewed, agent-drafted outbound message to carry an AI label. The message
must still truthfully identify Talli, its marketing purpose, and how personal
data is used. The agent must not imply that it is Kristian or another person.

Autonomous two-way conversation should remain **case-by-case approval only**.
If enabled later, tell the recipient before the interaction that they are
dealing with an automated assistant and provide a human contact route. This is
a forward-compatible Talli safeguard: EU AI Act article 50 requires people to
be informed when they directly interact with an AI system unless that is
obvious, although that Act is not yet Norwegian law. Source: [EU AI Act article
50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02024R1689-20260727).

## Agent operating boundary

The operational agent may, after Kristian approves the audience, templates,
limits, and tools:

- publish approved public posts where Talli has permission;
- contact partner organizations through genuine generic company inboxes;
- send approved messages to verified generic holding-AS inboxes if Kristian
  activates that fallback;
- acknowledge and sort inbound applications; and
- keep the compliance and suppression records described above.

The agent may not:

- choose a legally ambiguous channel by itself;
- acquire, scrape, enrich, or buy named-person lead data;
- send a message to a named person without recorded prior consent/request;
- negotiate promises, legal terms, future pricing, or filing guarantees;
- start a paid channel or paid service without Kristian's cost approval;
- continue after the operating limits or an objection; or
- begin autonomous two-way conversations without a fresh disclosure and human
  escalation decision.

## Recommended acquisition sequence

1. **Opt-in base:** publish the application page and privacy/participation text.
2. **Public reach:** let the agent publish clearly marked posts on Talli-owned
   channels and approved holding-company, founder, investor, and accounting
   communities.
3. **Partner reach:** let the agent approach associations, accountants, banks,
   investor communities, and founder networks through generic organizational
   inboxes and ask them to publish the public link. They should not send Talli a
   lead list.
4. **Referral loop:** give accepted applicants a shareable public link. If an
   incentive is used, make it fixed and transparent; the referred person must
   opt in directly.
5. **Generic-inbox fallback:** if the representative target is still unmet,
   Kristian may approve a small campaign to verified generic holding-AS
   inboxes using one approved message and one reminder, with no named-owner
   enrichment or tracking pixels.
6. **Re-check before launch:** review section 15, GDPR guidance, and Norway's AI
   Act status immediately before the first real outreach because the legal
   position and regulator guidance can change.

This route lets an agent do most of the recruitment work without cold-calling
or unlawfully cold-messaging named holding-company owners. It also keeps any
legally ambiguous channel behind Kristian's case-by-case approval.
